# Low-difficulty keyword opportunities for Everlittle

The easiest page-1 openings are example and template pages for letters and time capsules, age-specific birthday interview questions, grandparent letters, and a Qeepsake comparison page. No files were edited.

## How far to trust this

- **Demand:** evidence is Google autocomplete only (firefox client, en/US, run today). It proves people type the phrase, not how many. I found no third-party volume estimates, so none are quoted.
- **Difficulty:** verdicts come from the WebSearch tool's result set, which approximates Google but is not its exact ranking order. Spot-check the top 10 picks in an incognito Google window before building.
- **Unchecked SERPs:** rows marked "(SERP not checked)" are inferred from sibling queries.

One pattern across the checked SERPs: other tiny products already rank with this kind of content (time-capsule.io, sharewithleaf.com, timecapsule.com, memorymurals.com, ourtifacts.com, thetrove.family, lifeecho.org, heritagewhisper.com). That shows low-authority sites can get in, and that the comparison and "record stories" clusters are filling up.

## Clusters to avoid

- **"letter to my son" / "letter to my daughter" head terms:** autocomplete is dominated by song lyrics, Maya Angelou, Ta-Nehisi Coates and wedding-day letters. Only the occasion-specific long tails are worth it.
- **"letter to my unborn baby":** mixed intent (pregnancy loss "in heaven", a 2Pac song, "baby's father"). The SERP has baby-chick, timecapsule.com and literary pieces, and it overlaps your existing letters page.
- **Storyworth alternatives and "X vs Storyworth":** at least eight dedicated competitor pages (storii, simirity, memorymurals, tellmel, willow-stories, memoirji, heritagewhisper), and the intent is adult memoirs, not a child's archive.
- **"questions to ask your parents / grandparents before they die":** Time, Storyworth, The Good Trade, momjunction, Storii, Meminto and many startups. Use the kid-asks-grandparent angle (#24) instead.
- **"familyalbum vs tinybeans" and "familyalbum vs google photos":** FamilyAlbum's own blog ranks first for both, with memorymurals and thetrove behind it.
- **"digital baby book" / "baby book app":** app brands with exact-match pages (babynotebookapp twice, keeprcircle, Google Play), and it overlaps your baby memory journal page.
- **"how to save voicemails on iphone / android":** tech publishers own it and product fit is nil.
- **"sharenting" head term:** definitional and news intent (meaning, statistics, dangers). SERP not checked.
- **"baby milestones" generic:** developmental and medical intent. SERP not checked, but expect health publishers.
- **"what to write in a baby book" (unqualified):** the suggestions are all baby-shower gift inscriptions, which is the wrong intent.
- **"gmail for child" / "how to create an email account for a child":** parental-controls intent, not memory keeping.

## The 10 pages to build first

It is October, so the back-to-school pages (#14, #23, #37) can wait until about June; evergreen pages go first.

1. **Time capsule letter to child: examples and template** (#1, #38). Include 8–10 full sample letters by age and by writer (mum, dad, grandparent), plus a copyable template.
2. **First birthday time capsule: letters to open at 18** (#2, #3). Cover guest wording, prompt cards and what to ask relatives; link to the existing digital time capsule page.
3. **Birthday interview questions by age** (#7, #20). One hub with a section per age (1–6, 10, teens) and a printable; split into per-age pages if the hub gains traction.
4. **Letter to my baby on their first birthday** (#5). Son, daughter, from-dad and short versions.
5. **Letters from grandparents** (#8, #9). First-birthday letters and what to write to a first grandchild or newborn; link to the grandparents memory project page.
6. **18 letters for an 18th birthday** (#6). How to organise it, what each person writes, and how to collect them years ahead.
7. **Baby firsts checklist** (#12). 75–100 firsts grouped by month, printable.
8. **Qeepsake alternative, with a Tinybeans vs Qeepsake section** (#10, #11). This is the weakest comparison SERP I found.
9. **Baby book alternatives** (#16). An honest list of 8–10 options, including paper, with the product as one.
10. **An email account for your baby: how it works, pitfalls and alternatives** (#27). The current results are 2017-era news features, not practical guides.

Next in line: the birthday letter tradition (#13), legacy video questions (#18), questions for kids to ask grandparents (#24, before Grandparents Day next September), and the first-day-of-school letter and interview pages.
