# Everlittle parent survey

Status: published and verified in Tally on 21 September 2026. Public form: https://tally.so/r/dW7ekr. Editor: https://tally.so/forms/dW7ekr/edit. The plugin connection was unavailable, so creation used the Tally dashboard UI.

## Public form

Title: How do you keep your family's memories?

Introduction: A 5-minute survey for parents and caregivers aged 18+. We’re the team building Everlittle, and we want to understand what works and what doesn’t about keeping family memories. Honest answers, including “I don’t need another app,” help us decide what to build. Please don’t include your child’s name, exact birth date, or photos. Contact details are optional and will only be used if you agree to a research follow-up.

Use four short pages, a progress bar, Everlittle's cream background (#fffaf3), dark text (#352c29), and terracotta action color (#c54f37). Submit button: “Share my answers”. Do not enable partial-response capture or automated respondent emails.

### Page 1 — A little context

1. **How old is your youngest child?** Single choice, required.
   - Expecting my first child
   - Under 1 year
   - 1–2 years
   - 3–5 years
   - 6–12 years
   - 13 years or older
   - I’m a caregiver rather than a parent
   - None of these apply
   Logic: “None of these apply” ends the survey with thanks; do not include these responses in parent segments. Keep expecting parents as a separate segment.

2. **Which country do you live in?** Short text, required. Country only; no address.

3. **Where do you mainly keep family photos, videos, or stories today?** Single choice, required.
   - My phone’s photo library
   - Cloud storage or a shared album, such as Google Photos or iCloud
   - A messaging app, such as WhatsApp
   - Social media
   - A dedicated family or baby-memory app
   - A physical album, journal, or photo book
   - I don’t have a regular place
   - Something else
   Optional short text: **Which app or method do you use?** Show for “dedicated app” or “something else”.

### Page 2 — What actually happens

4. **Think of the last family moment you wanted to keep or share. What did you do with it?** Long text, optional. Hint: “A sentence or two is enough. If no example comes to mind, skip this.” No examples of a desired behavior: avoid priming answers.

5. **In the past 30 days, which of these caused you the most trouble?** Single choice, required.
   - Finding a particular photo or video again
   - Keeping the story or details behind a moment
   - Sharing with the right people
   - Getting memories from other family members
   - Making time to organize or record anything
   - Running out of storage
   - Something else
   - Nothing caused me trouble
   Optional long text shown unless “nothing”: **What happened, and what did you do about it?** This establishes consequences and workarounds, not just a feature vote.

6. **If you could improve just one thing about how you keep family memories, what would it be?** Short text, optional. Hint: “It’s also fine to say you’re happy with your current approach.” Capture the parent's own words before presenting product benefits.

### Page 3 — Alternatives and discovery

7. **In the past 12 months, what have you paid for to keep family memories?** Single choice, required. Hint: “If more than one, choose your most recent purchase.”
   - Extra cloud or photo storage
   - A family-memory or baby-journal app
   - Printed photos, an album, or a photo book
   - Something else
   - I haven’t paid for any of these
   - Prefer not to say
   Optional short text shown for a purchase: **What did you buy, and roughly what did it cost?** Hint: “Include the currency and whether it was one-time, monthly, or yearly. No receipt needed.” Actual spend informs pricing interviews; it does not establish willingness to pay for Everlittle.

8. **What would be your biggest reason not to try another family-memory app?** Single choice, required.
   - My current approach already works
   - Moving or uploading everything takes too much effort
   - I wouldn’t remember to use it
   - My family probably wouldn’t join
   - I’m unsure who could see or use our memories
   - I’m worried about losing access or getting our files back
   - The price
   - Something else
   - Nothing in particular

9. **Where did you most recently discover an app or service you actually tried for your family?** Single choice, required.
   - A friend or relative
   - A parent group or community
   - An Instagram post or Reel
   - An online ad
   - A YouTube video or podcast
   - A web or app-store search
   - Somewhere else
   - I haven’t tried one / can’t remember
   Optional short text: **Which group, creator, website, or search helped you find it?** Show unless “haven’t tried / can’t remember”. Hint: “A name or search phrase is enough; please don’t include private group conversations.”

### Page 4 — Everlittle and optional follow-up

10. **Before this survey, had you tried Everlittle?** Single choice, required.
    - I hadn’t heard of it
    - I’d heard of it but hadn’t visited
    - I visited but didn’t create an account
    - I created an account but haven’t saved a memory
    - I’ve saved at least one memory
    Conditional optional long text:
    - Visited / no account: **What was the main reason you didn’t create an account?**
    - Account / no memory: **What got in the way of saving your first memory?**
    - Saved a memory: **What, if anything, was confusing or disappointing when you used it?**
    Do not show an Everlittle pitch, free allowance, feature list, or price before these questions.

11. **May we contact you for a 20–30 minute conversation about your answers?** Single choice, required.
    - Yes, you may email me about a research conversation
    - No, thanks
    Show optional email only if Yes: **Email for research follow-up**. Hint: “Only for arranging research about these answers, not marketing emails. Leaving this blank is fine.” No name or phone field. No promise of an incentive until its amount and terms are set.

Hidden URL fields: utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, recruitment_source. These record recruitment context, not identity. Do not auto-detect a child's location or request child uploads.

Thank-you page: “Thank you. Your answers will help us decide what to improve and how to explain Everlittle more clearly. If you opted into a conversation and left an email, we may get in touch.”

## How answers drive decisions

| Evidence | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Child age + country + recent behavior | Compare parents of babies, toddlers, and older children; segment India and other countries rather than pooling them. |
| Current tool + concrete recent incident | Identify the actual alternative and the job Everlittle needs to do better. Build onboarding around familiar workflows. |
| Repeated problem + consequence/workaround | Prioritize a product change only when a problem recurs and people describe meaningful effort or loss. |
| Parent's own desired improvement | Draft landing headlines and ad hooks in their language; test those messages separately. |
| Recent purchases + amount/currency | Choose interview topics and price experiments. Do not convert stated or historical spend into a revenue forecast. |
| Biggest switching objection | Decide whether the next experiment concerns trust, imports, family invitations, reminders, or price. |
| Actual discovery source + named creator/search | Pick specific recruitment and marketing channels; compare with hidden recruitment source to spot sample bias. |
| Everlittle stage + reason for stopping | Separate unfamiliarity, landing-page objections, signup friction, and first-memory friction. |
| Research permission + email | Recruit follow-up conversations from contrasting segments, including people happy with their current approach. |

Analysis: report counts and denominators before percentages; keep non-parents and existing users separate; group open text by recurring themes and include de-identified examples. Small convenience samples are directional. A common theme is a hypothesis to test, not proof that a feature or channel will work. Select 3–5 contrasting interviews first, then choose one product experiment and one message experiment. Do not publish response excerpts containing personal identifiers.
