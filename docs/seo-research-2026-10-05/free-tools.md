## Top 5 to build first

**1. Time With Your Kids calculator** (`/tools/time-with-your-kids`), the link-earner

- **Inputs:** child's birthdate (add multiple kids). Optional toggles: custody or travel schedule, "counts from age 5 (when memories stick)".
- **Outputs:** summers, weekends, birthdays, bedtimes and Christmases left before 18, plus a 936-week grid with elapsed weeks filled in.
- **Honest second panel:** estimated share of lifetime in-person time already spent, with visible methodology. This is where we beat what ranks; nothing seen cites a source.
- **Shareable result:** a canvas-rendered image card ("312 weekends left with Maya") and a URL with the state encoded in the query string.
- **"Grandparents" tab:** visits left given grandparent age and visits per year. This captures `how many more times will i see my parents` without a separate build.
- **CTA:** "Make this weekend one she can open later."

**2. Class-of / school timeline calculator** (`/tools/graduation-year` plus static `/class-of/2044` and `/born-in/2026` pages), the traffic-earner

- **Inputs:** birthdate, US state (cutoff table), optional "started late / skipped a grade".
- **Outputs:** kindergarten start year, grade for each school year, high-school and college "Class of", child's age at graduation. With an optional parent birthdate, also the parent's age at each step, which answers `how old will i be when my kid graduates high school`.
- **Programmatic pages:** one static page per birth year and per class year. The born-in-year and class-of-year queries (`what year will my baby graduate if born in 2026`, `class of 2044 birth year`) are simple lookups a clean page can answer.
- **Shareable result:** a "Class of 2044" announcement card.
- **Better than what ranks:** no ads or filler, the state cutoff shown with its source and a "check your district" flag, and graduation year plus current grade in one view, which whatgradeismykid.com lacks.

**3. Birthday interview generator** (`/tools/birthday-interview`), the best product fit

- **Inputs:** child's name, the age they're turning, tone (sweet / funny / deep), number of questions (10/20/25).
- **Outputs:** an age-appropriate set with a fixed "core 10" that stay identical every year, so the comparison works, plus rotating extras. Shuffle and swap per question.
- **Printable:** a clean one-page print stylesheet with answer lines, name, age and date. Also a "first day of school" variant on the same engine.
- **Static sub-pages per age:** `/birthday-interview/3-year-old` through teens; each age has its own autocomplete suggestion.
- **Better than what ranks:** every result is a fixed list behind an email opt-in. Ours is instant, personalised and needs no email.
- **CTA:** "Record the answers in their own voice, and hear last year's next birthday."

**4. Family age timeline** (`/tools/how-old-will-i-be`), cheap and shares code with #2

- **Inputs:** any number of family members (name, birthdate, role: parent, child, grandparent).
- **Outputs:** a table and chart of everyone's age at each child milestone (starts school, 10, 13, 16, 18, graduation, 21, 30) and for any chosen year. Also the reverse view: "how old was I when I had my baby / how old were my parents when I was born".
- **Sibling age gap** as a built-in view (years, months, days; school-year overlap), which covers `sibling age gap calculator`.
- **Shareable result:** a URL with state, and a printable family age chart (`family age chart pdf` autocompletes).
- **CTA:** tied to the grandparent rows: "Grandpa will be 84 at her graduation. Ask him for a story now."

**5. Baby milestone dates calculator** (`/tools/baby-milestone-dates`), a small build with steady new-parent traffic

- **Inputs:** birthdate (optional name; optional due date to show adjusted age as a secondary line only).
- **Outputs:** exact age today (days, weeks, months), every monthly birthday with its weekday, day 100, half birthday, day 500, day 1,000, golden birthday, and "days you've been a parent".
- **Export:** an `.ics` calendar download generated client-side. None of the farms seen offer it.
- **Shareable result:** a "1,000 days of you" or "100 days" card.
- **Static sub-pages:** 100 days, half birthday and "how many days have I been a mom" (that last one has no dedicated tool).

Close sixth: **#6 the letter prompt generator**. It will earn the fewest links but is the most direct bridge to the product; I'd build it right after as the shared destination for the other tools' CTAs. **#7's kid-interviews-grandparent printable** is worth timing for Grandparents Day (first Sunday after Labor Day in the US).

## Accuracy pitfalls

- **School cutoffs.**
  - Most US states use about September 1, but dates range from late July to December/January. Some states (the search snippet names MA, NY and PA) leave it to districts.
  - Sources disagree in places: whatgradeismykid says July 1 for West Virginia, while another snippet says July 31 for Hawaii and Nebraska.
  - Transitional kindergarten (California), redshirting and retention all shift results.
  - Build the table from Education Commission of the States plus state education department pages, date-stamp it, and label the output "typical".
  - UK, Canada and Australia use different structures (`graduation year calculator uk` / `canada` / `australia` all autocomplete). Start US-only and say so.
- **"Time left" claims.**
  - The viral "75% by age 12" and "90% by 18" figures have no primary source I could find. Don't repeat them as fact.
  - A defensible basis is ATUS time-with-children by age. One analysis found via search cites about 7 waking hours a day in the preschool years falling to under 3.5 by senior year ([playfulbrains](https://playfulbrains.substack.com/p/you-only-have-13-years-of-playtime), secondary; verify against BLS tables). [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/who-do-americans-spend-time-with-over-their-lives) covers the adult side.
  - ATUS samples people 15 and over and measures a parent's time with any child in the household, not per child. State that as a limitation.
  - "18 summers" itself is contested ([pnmag](https://www.pnmag.com/parenthood/no-you-dont-only-have-18-summers-with-your-kids/)). Acknowledging that on the page is more credible and gives a better hook.
- **Grandparent mode.** Use SSA or CDC period life tables for remaining life expectancy at the grandparent's current age, not life expectancy at birth; the latter understates remaining years for older people. Present ranges and keep the tone gentle.
- **Date maths.**
  - Monthly birthdays for babies born on the 29th–31st, and Feb 29, need a stated end-of-month rule.
  - Half birthday can mean +6 calendar months or +182.5 days; pick one and say which.
  - 100-day customs differ on whether the birth day is day 0 or day 1 (Korean baek-il, Chinese 100 days, Japanese okuizome). Show both.
  - Use local-date arithmetic, not UTC milliseconds, to avoid off-by-one errors across DST and time zones.
- **Adjusted age** is medical-adjacent. Keep it as a secondary line with a "follow your pediatrician" note.
- **Moon phase, zodiac, birthstone** (if #9 is ever built). Moon phase by algorithm is accurate to about a day; zodiac cusp dates shift by year; several months have multiple birthstones and flowers.
- **Competition.** Direct competitors already publish in this content space: time-capsule.io, sharewithleaf.com, mylo.family, Storyworth, Meminto, heritagewhisper. Their pages are static articles; interactive tools are the gap.
