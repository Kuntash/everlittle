# Everlittle: first five parent interviews

Draft for review. No recruitment posted, participants contacted, platform purchased or incentives promised.

## Recommendation

Recruit five adult parents of children aged 1–3 for 30-minute remote interviews. You do not need
newborn parents or people in your personal network. Toddlers give parents recent examples of
photos, first words, family sharing and memories they have struggled to find.

Start in the country you intend to sell into. The current ads target the US, so US parents are the
initial recommendation. Do not mix markets in a five-person round and assume willingness to pay
transfers between them.

Pay for their time regardless of whether they like Everlittle, sign up or purchase. Proposed US
incentive: $30 for 30 minutes; five completed interviews = $150 in incentives, plus recruitment fees.
This is a starting budget recommendation, not a platform quote. A follow-up task needs its own
agreed time and compensation. Do not count paid research participation as organic activation.

  incentives; automatic incentive distribution has an additional processing fee. Confirm the
  consumer audience quote and available parent targeting before purchasing.
  https://www.userinterviews.com/recruit
  https://www.userinterviews.com/pricing
- **Respondent:** alternative participant panel with consumer recruitment and parent targeting in
  its incentive calculator. Confirm audience availability and recruitment fees separately from the
  participant incentive.
  https://www.respondent.io/pricing
  https://www.respondent.io/recruiting-cost-calculator
- **Lower-cost route:** a short screener shared, with moderator permission, in local parenting
  groups, or forwarded by a family photographer or nursery contact. The form collects applicants;
  it does not itself provide distribution. Start with one channel rather than mass posting.

Sources checked September 21, 2026. Neither platform guarantees the exact requested sample before
screening. Use the platform's screener when using its panel; a separate form is unnecessary.

## Draft invitation

**How do you save and share family memories?**

We’re speaking with parents of toddlers about how they keep photos, little sayings and other
family memories. Join a 30-minute video call and receive $30 as a thank-you. No purchase needed,
and you do not need to use Everlittle. You can use sample photos; your child does not need to
participate. Apply through this short form. We’ll contact selected participants to arrange a time.

Publish only after confirming the incentive budget and who will distribute it.

## Two-minute screener

Intro: “This form helps us choose participants for a parent research interview. Applying does not
guarantee a session. We’ll use your answers and email only to arrange this research.”

1. Are you 18 or older? **Yes / No**
2. Which country do you live in? **Country**
3. What age groups are your children in? **Under 1 / 1–3 / 4–6 / 7+ / I am not a parent or guardian**
4. Which do you currently use to keep or share family memories? **Phone camera roll / Shared photo
   albums / WhatsApp or messaging / Social media / Printed album or journal / Family memory app /
   Something else / None**. Select all that apply.
5. Tell us about the last time you saved or shared a memory of your child. **One or two sentences**
6. Have you tried a new way of organizing or sharing these memories in the last six months?
   **Yes / No**. Optional: What did you try?
7. Can you join a 30-minute call and try a website on your phone? **Yes / No**
8. Where can we contact you? **First name, email and timezone**
9. May we contact you to schedule this research? **Yes / No**

Ask separately about recording at the start of the call; participation should not require it.
Do not ask for children's names, exact birth dates, photos or identity documents in this form.
Do not add applicants to marketing emails without a separate opt-in.

Selection: adults in the chosen country with a child aged 1–3 and a credible recent example.
Seek different current approaches: roughly two camera-roll/messaging users, two shared-album
users, and one journal or memory-app user. Include someone satisfied with their current approach.
Do not select only people who say they love memory keeping or would pay for an app.

## 30-minute interview

**0–3 minutes:** Explain that the product is being tested, not the parent. Confirm the incentive,
recording choice and ability to stop. Use sample content for the walkthrough.

**3–15 minutes: recent behavior, before showing Everlittle**

- Tell me about the last photo, phrase or moment you wanted to keep.
- What did you do with it? Who else saw it?
- When did you last try to find an older memory? What happened?
- What have you tried to make this easier? Why did you keep using it or stop?
- Have you spent money on this? What did you buy, and what made it worth it?

Follow concrete examples. Avoid “Would you use an app that…?” and “Do you think this is a good idea?”

**15–25 minutes: unassisted walkthrough**

Show the landing page briefly, then ask: “What do you think this does? What is free? What costs money?”

Task: “Imagine you want to keep a photo and the story behind it, then share it with a grandparent.
Show me what you would do.” Let them navigate and explain their expectations. Use a designated
research environment/account and sample photo. Do not steer them toward the correct button.
Note unclear labels, pauses, errors, privacy concerns and abandoned steps. Do not collect passwords.

**25–30 minutes: fit and next step**

- What would you use this for, if anything, that you cannot do comfortably today?
- What would stop you trusting this with your memories?
- Which part could you skip entirely?
- Looking at the real free and paid options, what would you choose and why?

Optional, separate consent: ask whether they want to try the free product independently and be
contacted once in a week. Their interview payment must not depend on accepting.

## What to record

Use participant IDs and brief notes: current workflow; specific recent problem; existing spending;
landing-page interpretation; task outcome; major friction; privacy objection; free/paid choice.
Keep contact details separate from the synthesis. Summarize repeated problems and contrary examples.

Five interviews identify useful patterns; they do not estimate a market conversion rate. A compliment
or paid task completion is not proof of demand. Independent return use and voluntary payment are
stronger evidence. Keep research-tagged traffic out of the organic acquisition funnel.
